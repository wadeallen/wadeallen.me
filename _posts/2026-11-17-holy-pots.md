We are excited about Thanksgiving. We have family coming in from across the country and are looking forward to spending quality time with them. As a family, we'll be cooking together. We've reserved a turkey from a local farm and will be carefully preparing it along with several family-favorite side dishes. I'm getting hungry just thinking about it. 

As I prepare this week's sermon, we've reached the last chapter of Zechariah. It's been quite a journey through this book. There's lots of imagery describing how God is working and moving among the people then. It also describes how God is going to act in the future, through Jesus. 

At the same time, it is Christ the King Sunday. Zechariah 14 and Christ the King fit together well. Throughout this chapter, God is painting a picture of all that He's going to do. He uses the phrase "on that day" over and over again. Near the end of the chapter, Zechariah describes ordinary items being inscribed with priestly words. 

> (Zechariah 14:20 NIV) On that day HOLY TO THE LORD will be inscribed on the bells of the horses, and the cooking pots in the LORD's house will be like the sacred bowls in front of the altar.

Horses and cooking pots were used every day in the ancient world. This inscription was originally on the high priest's turban. Here Zechariah describes ordinary items becoming holy. In other words, the kingdom of God is not limited to holy places. In fact, everything is holy in God's kingdom.

Zechariah goes on to say in verse 21:

> (Zechariah 14:21 NIV) Every pot in Jerusalem and Judah will be holy to the LORD Almighty...

And the second part of verse 21:

> (Zechariah 14:21 NIV) ...and all who come to sacrifice will take some of the pots and cook in them.

Worship makes its way into the pots, into daily life.

This is worth considering. I wonder how we see our everyday routines. Do we consider every aspect of our lives holy, even the ordinary things, or do we limit God's presence to church? 

As we approach the Thanksgiving season, let's remember that God's presence is everywhere. It's not just at church on Sunday morning. It's in our homes, our workplaces, our neighborhoods. Every conversation, every interaction is an opportunity for God to work and move. Might we be mindful of this dynamic, realizing that Christ is King everywhere? 

Back in verse 21, the worshipers leave to cook meals. We'll be doing the same thing in the coming days. We'll gather for Christ the King Sunday and then cook on Thursday. Let's bring the two together, letting the rule and reign of Jesus reach everything we do.
